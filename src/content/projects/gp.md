<h2> Essay question: Should there be any control over the production of energy when the need for it is so great? </h2>

Yes to control. 
Firms, government 

Stakeholders: 
* Fossil fuel producers -> control supply and other energy -> maximize profits -> OPEC 
* Environmental activists -> produce less fossil fuels, more clean energy -> for sustainability 

These 2 stakeholders have different perspectives. Producers control production to limit supply and increase price of energy for profit maximization. Environmental activists mainly prioritize the sustainability of energy production. 

Political leverage, Safety concerns (nuclear energy) and manpower considerations. 
Even with brilliant minds (talent), there is not enough people to run the power plants. 

Even though the need is very great, the control of energy is more important as safety is prioritized. Hence stakeholders often reduce the need of energy to solve the issue instead. 

Control fossil fuel usage as a form of buffer for future generations. 

Final Stand: Yes, for self serving reasons as well as sustainability reasons.

<h2>Essay question: No country should sacrifice its economic development in favour preserving the environment. Discuss </h2>

For absolute questions like these, skip the thinking process and assume that there definitely should have countries. 

Pollution from one country would spread to neighbors and maybe even globally. 

Short run - Long run analysis

If you get a country to slowdown economy for environment, they will probably find another way to improve economy:
Economic opportunity of the production of green technology. China being one of the main drivers. Preserving environment benefits welfare of citizens may be of more importance than economic development 

Countries that require resources from the environment for economic development -> Primary industries 

There will always be environmental degradation of some kind, but there are existing countries/firms that are trying to shift from fossil fuel to clean energy sources that are obtained from the environment. 

Eg. US is a country that does not want to rely on other countries for energy, for political purposes. They ave resorted to environmentally unfriendly practices like fracking for oil. Damage and loss funding - richer countries should pay the poorer countries to preserve the environment. 

For countries with the innovation and economic ability, they can protect the environment while developing the economy through different means. 

Singapore has always cleared land for economic development. However, now there are huge efforts to prevent environmental degradation, through more creative ways to build infrastructure such that there are still patches of land for nature to thrive. 

<h2>Essay question: Since the extinction of species is a natural phenomenon, is there any point in trying to prevent it? </h2>

Given the immense scale and complexity of nature, it is in practice difficult to enact the level of legislation needed to meaningfully prevent the extinction of species. 

Ex. Convention on International trade in Endangered Species (CITES) 

Historical extinction rate was 1-5 species per million species per year. currently 100-10000 times higher. 

Is there any <b>point</b>: 
- value -> intangibles or tangibles
- usefulness 

Species extinction: 
1. Save all
2. Be selective
3. Ignore
4. Kill them all 

Option 1,4: Not recommended -> too difficult to catch em all

Option 3: Pragmatic, but not enough content to write since its quite absolute. Use as counter argument. There are bigger fish to fry

Option 2: Best stand as there is more room for elaboration. 

<b>Be selective: </b>

- Charismatic megafauna
- Essential to the ecosystems
- Distinctive emblems 
- Easy to save
- With known benefits
- Cultural or political significance 
- Those whose demise are caused by us 

Most people start with the most feasible species to save. However, species with significant importance still receive immense efforts to be saved. 

Ex. Pandas in China. The return on investment is very high, as the cost to preserve them is less than the revenue generated from tourism etc. Political leverage, unique to China.
